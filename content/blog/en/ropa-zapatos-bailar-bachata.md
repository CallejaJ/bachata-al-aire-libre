One of the most common questions from my new students is: **"What should I wear to dance bachata?"**

The good news is that you don't need special clothing or a big budget. But there are some recommendations that will make your experience much more comfortable.

## The Golden Rule: Comfort

First and foremost: **if you don't feel comfortable, you won't dance well**.

Your clothes and shoes need to allow you to:

Move freely (especially your hips and legs)

Turn without getting tangled

Sweat without feeling uncomfortable

Feel confident and attractive

## For Classes (Casual Setting)

### Women

**Top/T-shirt**:

🎶 Comfortable T-shirt or sports top

🎶 One that doesn't ride up when you raise your arms

🎶 Wide straps (more comfortable than thin ones)

🎶 Avoid very low necklines (you'll be constantly adjusting)

**Trousers/Skirt**:

🎶 Sports leggings (the most common choice)

🎶 Soft-fabric trousers (not tight jeans)

🎶 Dance skirt (one that lets you open your legs)

🎶 Sports shorts (especially in summer in Málaga)

**What to AVOID**:

➖ Long or very voluminous skirts

➖ Tight jeans (restrict hip movement)

➖ Very loose clothing that gets tangled

➖ Party dresses (save those for socials)

### Men

**T-shirt**:

🎶 Cotton or sports T-shirt

🎶 Casual polo shirt

🎶 Informal shirt (one or two buttons undone)

🎶 Something that absorbs sweat well

**Trousers**:

🎶 Chinos or fabric trousers

🎶 Smart sports trousers

🎶 Jeans that aren't too tight (if they're comfortable, fine)

**What to AVOID**:

➖ Sleeveless vests (not well regarded in many schools)

➖ Shorts (some venues don't allow them)

➖ Very informal sportswear (tracksuit-type)

➖ Shirts with buttons that come undone easily

## For Socials and Parties (More Dressed Up)

### Women

**Dress**:

🎶 Fitted dress that flatters your figure

🎶 Mid-length (at the knee) or short

🎶 With some stretch

🎶 Moderate neckline (remember you'll be dancing very close)

**Alternatives**:

🎶 Nice top + dance skirt

🎶 Bodysuit + smart trousers

🎶 Jumpsuit (if it allows movement)

**Recommended colours**:

Black, red, blue, emerald green (stand out on the floor)

Avoid white (shows sweat)

**Accessories**:

➖ Avoid long necklaces (they get in the way)

➖ Avoid noisy bracelets (they're distracting)

🎶 Small or medium earrings (yes)

🎶 Simple ring (yes)

### Men

**Shirt**:

🎶 Dress shirt (short or long sleeves)

🎶 Solid colours or subtle pattern

🎶 Well ironed

🎶 Breathable fabric

**Trousers**:

🎶 Chinos or dress trousers

🎶 Dark colours (hide sweat)

🎶 Matching belt

**Style**:

🎶 Long sleeves rolled up to the elbow (classic Latin look)

🎶 Second button undone

🎶 Everything ironed and clean

## Shoes: The MOST Important Part

Shoes are probably **the most important** part of your whole outfit.

### For Women

**Option 1: Latin Dance Shoes** (ideal)

Heel height 5–8 cm (beginners: 5–6 cm)

Suede or soft leather sole

Ankle strap (Mary Jane style)

Cushioned insole

**Price**: 40–80€

**Brands**: Diamant, Werner Kern, Supadance, Freed

**Option 2: Regular Heels** (alternative)

Block heel or wedge (more stable)

Maximum 5–7 cm height

With ankle strap or T-bar

NOT rubber sole (doesn't slide properly)

**Option 3: Flats** (for casual classes)

Ballet or jazz flats

Canvas flat shoes

Trainers ONLY if they have a flat, flexible sole

**What you should NEVER wear**:

➖ High stilettos (you'll fall)

➖ Open sandals (risk to your toes)

➖ Brand new shoes (guaranteed blisters)

➖ Boots (prevent ankle movement)

➖ Flip-flops (obviously)

### For Men

**Option 1: Latin Dance Shoes** (ideal)

Suede sole

Small Cuban heel (2–3 cm)

Light and flexible

Oxford or Derby style

**Price**: 50–90€

**Option 2: Dress Shoes** (alternative)

Soft leather loafers

Oxford with thin sole

Suede shoes

NOT hard rubber sole

**Option 3: Trainers** (only for very casual classes)

Canvas trainers like Vans

Minimalist trainers

NEVER thick-soled sports trainers

**What you should NEVER wear**:

➖ Shoes with thick rubber soles (you can't turn properly)

➖ Heavy boots

➖ Running trainers

➖ Shoes with extreme grip

## Considerations for the Málaga Climate

### Summer (May – September)

**Problem**: It gets VERY hot, and dancing makes it hotter

**Solution**:

Lightweight cotton clothing or technical fabrics

Light colours for classes (reflect heat)

Spare T-shirts (change between class and social)

Deodorant and small towel in your bag

**For outdoor classes** (like mine):

Cap or hat for before/after class

Sun cream

Breathable clothing

### Winter (October – April)

**Problem**: Can be cold when you arrive, but you'll heat up dancing

**Solution**:

Layering system

Light jacket you can leave in the cloakroom

Arrive wrapped up, dance in lighter clothing

## Practical Tips from a Veteran

### Looking After Your Dance Shoes

🎶 Use them ONLY for dancing (the sole wears down)

🎶 Carry them in a bag, change when you arrive

🎶 Brush the suede sole after use

🎶 Alternate between two pairs if you dance a lot

### Hygiene Is Key

🎶 Clean clothes ALWAYS (seems obvious but...)

🎶 Deodorant before class

🎶 Spare T-shirt if you sweat a lot

🎶 Gum or mints after eating

🎶 Small towel in your bag

### What to Carry in Your Dance Bag

**Essentials**:

Dance shoes

Deodorant

Small towel

Water

Spare T-shirt

**Useful extras**:

Mini blister kit (plasters, Compeed)

Hair clips (women with long hair)

Hair band

Small perfume/cologne

Energy snack

## Common Beginner Mistakes

### Mistake #1: Wearing new shoes on the night of your first social

**Result**: Terrible blisters

🎶 **Solution**: Break in shoes during class, not at a social

### Mistake #2: Wearing very tight clothing "to look good"

**Result**: You can't move, you dance badly

🎶 **Solution**: Prioritise comfort over looking good

### Mistake #3: Very high heels out of vanity

**Result**: Twisted ankles, pain, can't dance

🎶 **Solution**: Beginners: maximum 6 cm

### Mistake #4: Not thinking about sweat

**Result**: Soaked clothing, uncomfortable partners

🎶 **Solution**: Technical fabrics, colours that hide it

## Minimum Budget to Get Started

### Budget Option (50–80€)

Leggings/trousers from Decathlon: 15–20€

Sports T-shirt: 10–15€

Canvas shoes or basic heels: 25–45€

**Total**: Under 80€

### Standard Option (150–200€)

Clothes for class (2–3 outfits): 60–80€

Outfit for socials: 40–60€

Good dance shoes: 50–70€

**Total**: Around 180€

### Premium Option (300–400€+)

Multiple outfits

2 pairs of specialist shoes

Accessories

Clothes for different occasions

## My Personal Recommendation

For **absolute beginners** who aren't sure yet if they'll stick with it:

➖ Don't spend on expensive shoes yet

🎶 Use comfortable sportswear you already have

🎶 Low heels or flat shoes

🎶 After 4–6 classes, invest in proper dance shoes

For **tourists** staying 1–2 weeks:

➖ Don't buy specialist shoes

🎶 Bring comfortable summer clothes

🎶 One semi-smart outfit for a social

🎶 Comfortable regular heels/shoes

For **residents** who are serious about it:

🎶 Invest in good shoes from month 2–3 onwards

🎶 Several outfits to rotate

🎶 A special outfit for important socials

## For Outdoor Classes (Like Mine)

Outdoor classes have special considerations:

**Surface**:

Park/grass: Flat shoes or trainers

Paved area: Soft-soled shoes

**Weather**:

Very light clothing

Sun protection

Cap/hat for before/after

**Style**:

Very casual, sporty

Nobody expects you to be dressed up

Total comfort

## Conclusion

The right clothes and shoes won't make you a better dancer, but the **wrong** ones will definitely make you dance **worse**.

My advice: start simple and comfortable, invest more once you're sure bachata is for you.

And remember: in Málaga it's warm, so prioritise light, breathable clothing.

See you on the floor! 💃🕺
